Of the 1,762 companies in Y Combinator's 2015–2020 batches, now about 6 to 12 years old, roughly a quarter landed in each of four outcomes: 27% shut down, 22% exited or became a top company, 26% are active with 25 or more staff, and 25% are active with fewer.

The strongest pattern is business model. Within the same batch year, consumer companies had 1.92 times the odds of shutting down compared with B2B companies. World events line up with how many companies YC funded and what they built, but this data can't show whether those events changed who survived.

This covers YC only, so it doesn't describe startups in general. YC is highly selective, and its companies get funding and a network most startups don't.

## How outcomes are defined

The data covers 4,823 companies from the Winter 2015 to Fall 2025 batches, taken from a daily snapshot of YC's public directory on November 2, 2026. Each company falls into one of four groups:

| Outcome | Rule | All batches |
|---|---|---|
| Exited or top company | Public, acquired, or on YC's top-company list | 613 |
| Active, 25+ staff | Listed as active with a team of 25 or more | 901 |
| Active, under 25 staff | Listed as active with a smaller team or no team size | 2,499 |
| Shut down | Listed as inactive | 810 |

Only 18 of the 613 in the first group are top companies that are still active. The other 595 were acquired or went public. The headline figures use the 2015–2020 batches because later companies have had less time to exit or fail.

## Outcomes by batch year

<YcChart chart="outcomes" caption="Number under each year: companies in that year's batches. Younger batches have had less time to exit or shut down, so compare years with age in mind." />

The 2021 batches stand out. Of those companies, 42% are still active with fewer than 25 staff, against 29% of the 2020 batches, while 11% have exited or become top companies, against 18%. One extra year of age seems unlikely to explain all of that, because the same share barely moved from 2019 to 2020 (32% to 29%).

The 2021 batches also have a lower shutdown share, 18% against 24% for 2020. A low number here may just mean less time has passed, so it isn't evidence that those companies are healthier. The data has no funding rounds or revenue, so it can't say why they stayed small.

## Funding cycles and world events

<YcChart chart="funding" caption="Global venture funding from Crunchbase, using the latest figure in the reports listed under Sources. 2019 is left out because no source for it could be confirmed." />

YC's yearly intake and global venture funding moved together from 2020 to 2025, with a correlation of 0.84. That rests on six years, and it falls to 0.71 without 2021, so read it as a pattern and not a rule. YC sets its own batch sizes and moved to four batches a year in this period.

| Batch year | Events | YC companies | Shift in that year's batches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | COVID-19; YC moves online | 438 | 29% outside US/Canada |
| 2021 | Near-zero rates; record venture funding | 725 | Largest year; 42% outside US/Canada |
| 2022 | Rate hikes; FTX collapse; YC returns in person from Summer 2022 | 631 | Fintech peaks at 21%; crypto at 5% |
| 2023 | ChatGPT wave; Silicon Valley Bank failure | 494 | AI 56%; fintech 10%; crypto 1% |
| 2024 | YC runs its first fall batch | 590 | AI 66%; 14% outside US/Canada |
| 2025 | Four batches a year; AI takes about half of global venture funding | 621 | AI 61%; 15% outside US/Canada |

## What founders built

<YcChart chart="themes" caption="Tags and locations are current, not as founded. Outside US/Canada includes companies with an unknown location, 1–7% of each year's batches from 2019 on." />

- **AI:** AI-tagged companies went from 35% of the 2022 batches to 56% in 2023 and 66% in 2024. ChatGPT launched in November 2022. It's too early to judge outcomes for these companies.
- **Crypto and fintech:** after the FTX collapse and rate hikes of 2022, crypto fell from 5% to 1% of the batches and fintech from 21% to 10%. Crypto is a small group, 89 companies in total.
- **Location:** companies outside the US and Canada rose from 25% in 2019 to 42% in 2021, while YC ran online. The share stayed near 40% into Winter 2022, then fell to 37% in Summer 2022, 26% in Winter 2023, and about 15% in 2024. That timing fits YC's return to in-person batches, but it doesn't prove the cause, and funding fell over the same period.
- **Healthcare:** its share went from 13% in 2019 to 16% in 2020 and 14% in 2021, so there's no large pandemic surge in this data.

## Which bets held up

These tables cover the 2015–2020 batches.

<YcChart chart="industries" caption="Industries with at least 40 companies, sorted by the combined share in the first two outcome columns." />

- **Consumer** had the highest shutdown share, 41%. Social apps were higher still at 54% (48 companies), the most of any sub-industry with at least 25 companies.
- **Fintech** had the lowest shutdown share, 17%, level with the much smaller Education group, and the largest combined share exited or at 25+ staff, 61%. Most of that comes from staff growth. Its 21% exit-or-top share is below B2B's 28%.
- **Healthcare** shut down at about the same rate as B2B, 24% against 25%, but fewer companies reached scale: 41% against 54%. Headcount and exits may undercount companies on long development timelines. In therapeutics, 67% are still under 25 staff (30 companies).

<YcChart chart="regions" caption="Regions with at least 20 companies, sorted the same way. Location is the company's current one." />

Companies outside the US often show more staff growth but fewer exits. LatAm's exit-or-top share is 14% and India's is 17%, against 24% for US/Canada, while 47% of LatAm and 40% of India companies are active with 25+ staff, against 23%. Hiring 25 people likely costs less outside the US, so this measure may flatter those regions.

Read the shutdown column with care. The Other/Unknown group shows 56% shut down, which suggests some dead companies lost their location in the directory. If so, shutdown shares in the named regions are understated.

## Same-batch comparison

To compare companies at the same age, a logistic regression was run on the 3,612 companies in the 2015–2023 batches, with a separate baseline for each batch year. An odds ratio above 1 means more likely than the comparison group.

<YcChart chart="odds" caption="Lines show 95% intervals. Region results lean on headcount. With 28 comparisons here, one or two could clear p under 0.05 by chance." />

- **Consumer** is the clearest result on both measures: 1.92× the odds of shutting down and 0.54× the odds of exiting or growing. A separate check on only the 2015–2020 batches gave 1.93× for shutting down.
- **Healthcare** had 0.58× the odds of exiting or growing. Its lower shutdown odds (0.71×) did not hold up in the 2015–2020 check, which gave 0.91× with an interval that crosses 1.
- **LatAm** had 1.96× the odds of exiting or growing, with the headcount caveat above.
- **AI-tagged** companies had 0.71× the odds of shutting down, but this is likely inflated. Tags are current, so a company that pivoted into AI and survived counts as AI, while one that died before pivoting doesn't.
- **Crypto-tagged** companies had 1.68× the odds of shutting down, with an interval that just crosses 1, so the result isn't clear.

The regression can't compare business model with world events. It holds batch year constant, and a batch year combines conditions at founding with company age.

## Limits of this analysis

- **Coverage:** the directory only includes publicly launched companies with pages on YC's website, so companies that never launched are missing.
- **Label lag:** YC's status labels can lag. Some small "active" companies may be gone, so the true shutdown rate is probably higher than shown.
- **Exits:** "acquired" includes acqui-hires, which can be soft failures. The data has no sale prices.
- **Current data:** tags, locations, and team sizes are the latest listed, not as founded, and team sizes may be stale. 88 companies have no team size.
- **Success is a proxy:** the data has no revenue, valuation, or funding, so "growing" only means 25 or more staff.
- **Few data points:** world events line up with whole batch years, about ten in all. These are correlations, not causes.
- **Other startup programs:** India's Startup India portal was not used. It records registrations rather than outcomes and couldn't be accessed for this analysis.

The analysis was run with Claude, an AI model, on the public data set. The figures were recalculated from the company-level file before publication, and the regression was refit without its marketplace and hardware tags, which moved the consumer and healthcare odds ratios by 0.02 or less.

## Frequently asked questions

**What share of YC startups shut down?** Of the 1,762 companies in the 2015–2020 batches, 27% are marked inactive. The true figure is probably higher, because the labels can lag and another 25% are still listed as active with fewer than 25 staff.

**Which kinds of YC startups shut down most often?** Consumer companies. In the 2015–2020 batches, 41% of them shut down, against 25% of B2B companies. Social apps were highest among sub-industries with at least 25 companies, at 54%.

**Did world events change which YC startups survived?** This data can't answer that. A batch year sets both the conditions a company started in and how old it is now. World events do line up with how many companies YC funded and what they worked on.
